Session by session

Each session is two hours. Together they cover the topics on the FAA Part 107 knowledge test.

  1. Session 1

    How drones fly

    • Basic aerodynamics: lift, drag, and thrust
    • The fundamentals of drone flight control: roll, bank, and yaw
    • Sample drone configurations and missions, such as surveillance, monitoring, and speed
    • The pros and cons of each configuration

  2. Session 2

    Drone systems and safety

    • An unmanned aircraft system (UAS) and its components
    • Remote control
    • Maneuvers
    • Flying safely

  3. Session 3

    Weather and airspace

    • The atmosphere and how it affects flight
    • The airspace: where and how drones are allowed to fly

  4. Session 4

    FAA test practice

    • Working through FAA Part 107 practice problems

  5. Session 5

    Test sign-up and review

    • Registering for the FAA Part 107 test
    • A review of the whole course

About the FAA Part 107 test

Part 107 is the FAA's Remote Pilot certificate, the license for flying drones for work. This course prepares you for its knowledge test.

  • You take the test separately, at an FAA-approved testing center.
  • The FAA's test fee is separate from this course.
  • You must be at least 16 years old to earn the certificate.

The contest

Put your skills to work in three flying challenges on campus.

  • You fly quadcopters that we provide. No need to bring your own.
  • All challenges take place in a 20 × 20 × 10 ft flight space.
  • Every pilot gets three tries, and the best score counts.
  • Full rules and scoring will be shared before contest day.

A. Speed delivery

A minimum-time delivery run. Fly the route as fast as you can, touching down on each landing pad along the way.

B. Precision corridor

Fly through a specified corridor, including a hoop, with skill and control.

C. Endurance loop

A dynamic, repeating flight pattern. Keep your drone flying the loop for as long as you can.
