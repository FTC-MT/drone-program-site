Take Flight drone skills contest

Contest rules and scoring

Three events test three different piloting skills: precision landing, obstacle navigation, and drone soccer-style maneuvering. Every event is scored out of 100 points, and your overall score is the average of all three.

These are draft rules. All dimensions, time limits, and point values are provisional until our staff finish safety and feasibility testing. Official rules, including tie-breakers and penalties, will be published before contest day.

Event A

Precision Delivery and Landing

Fly a set route with three touchdowns along the way, as accurately and as quickly as you can.

Requirements

  • Take off from START, land on pads A, B, and C in order, and finish on the final pad.
  • Hold a controlled landing on each pad for at least two seconds before taking off again.
  • Finish within 120 seconds, stay inside the flight area, and avoid unsafe contact.
  • Landing pads will be about 18–24 inches across.

Scoring (100 points)

CriterionPointsHow it's judged
Completion time35Faster complete routes score more (see time score)
Landing accuracy35How close to the center of each pad you land
Touchdown sequence20Correct order, and holding each landing for two seconds
Stability and safety10Controlled flight; set deductions for contact

An incomplete route gets no completion-time points.

Event B

Obstacle Corridor Navigation

Fly through four gates in order, efficiently, precisely, and without touching them.

Requirements

  • Pass through four gates in the set order and direction.
  • The course includes side-to-side offsets and one modest change in height. Land at FINISH.
  • Gate openings will be about 30–36 inches, sized to fit the protected drone.
  • Finish within 120 seconds. Touching an obstacle costs a penalty, published in advance.

Scoring (100 points)

CriterionPointsHow it's judged
Gate passages4010 for each gate passed correctly
Completion time25Faster complete routes score more (see time score)
Passage accuracy25Up to 6.25 per gate, based on marked zones in the opening
Stability and safety10Controlled flight; deductions for contact

A gate counts only if the whole drone passes through the opening in the required direction.

Event C

Drone Soccer Skills Challenge

Three 30-second stages test goal shooting, avoiding interference, and timing your move past a "defender." This is a skills challenge, not a full-contact drone soccer match.

Stage 1 · 30 seconds · 20 points

Precision goal shooting

Fly all the way through an open goal without touching the frame.

Stage 2 · 30 seconds · 30 points

Shooting with interference

Fly around a soft, stationary obstacle, then through the goal.

Stage 3 · 30 seconds · 35 points

Timed interception response

Watch a slow, padded barrier move back and forth, then pass through the goal at the right moment. The barrier can't strike or trap your drone.

Scoring (100 points)

CriterionPoints
Stage 1: precision goal passage20
Stage 2: avoidance and goal passage30
Stage 3: timed response and goal passage35
Overall stability and safety15

Every pilot gets the same number of goal attempts in each stage, and the moving barrier always starts in the same position with the same timing. Two full official runs; your best score counts.

Overall scoring

Overall score = (Event A + Event B + Event C) ÷ 3

Each event is worth up to 100 points, so the overall score is also out of 100.

How the time score works

In Events A and B, part of your score depends on speed. Before contest day we'll publish a benchmark time for each event. Finish at or under the benchmark and you get all the time points. Take longer and you get a share: for example, taking twice the benchmark time earns half the time points. You only get time points if you complete the route.

For the curious: time points = maximum × min(1, benchmark time ÷ your time).

Safety first

  • Only one drone flies at a time, and every drone has propeller protection.
  • Every pilot gets a safety briefing and supervised practice before competing.
  • Staff can disarm a drone immediately in an emergency, and judges watch from protected positions.
  • Dangerous or uncontrolled flight ends the attempt immediately.
  • If the equipment fails, only the judges can authorize a re-flight, under the same policy for everyone.
